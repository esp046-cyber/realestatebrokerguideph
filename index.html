Getting Started as a Real Estate Broker

Welcome to the Philippine Real Estate Industry! This guide will help you navigate your career as a licensed real estate broker in 2026.

Essential First Steps

  1. Ensure your PRC license is active and updated
  2. Register with DHSUD (Department of Human Settlements and Urban Development)
  3. Secure Professional Tax Receipt (PTR)
  4. Get Professional Indemnity Insurance
  5. Join a reputable brokerage firm or establish your own

Required Documents to Keep

Valid PRC License (renew every 3 years)
Professional Tax Receipt (annual)
DHSUD Registration Certificate
Professional Indemnity Insurance
Business Permits (if operating own firm)
BIR Registration (TIN)

Daily Responsibilities

  • Client consultations and property viewings
  • Market research and property valuation
  • Negotiating deals between buyers and sellers
  • Preparing contracts and legal documents
  • Following up on transactions and closings
  • Networking and building client relationships
Pro Tip: Build your personal brand early. Create social media profiles showcasing your expertise in specific areas like BGC condos, Makati commercial properties, or provincial land development.

Licensing & Professional Development

PRC License Requirements (2026)

  • Must be Filipino citizen or permanent resident
  • At least 18 years of age
  • College degree or completion of specific real estate courses
  • Pass the Real Estate Broker Licensure Examination
  • No conviction of crimes involving moral turpitude

License Renewal Process

  1. Complete 45 CPD units within 3-year cycle
  2. Submit renewal application to PRC 3 months before expiry
  3. Pay renewal fees (approximately ₱450-650)
  4. Submit updated 2x2 photos
  5. Present proof of CPD compliance
CPD Requirements: 45 units total - including ethics, real estate laws, taxation, property management, and market trends.

Continuing Professional Development

You must earn 45 CPD units every 3 years:

  • Core subjects: 30 units (Real Estate Law, Ethics, Taxation)
  • Elective subjects: 15 units (Marketing, Property Management, Technology)
  • Attend PRC-accredited seminars, workshops, or online courses
Warning: Practicing without an active license can result in fines up to ₱100,000 and imprisonment. Always keep your license current!

Professional Organizations to Join

  • Philippine Association of Real Estate Boards (PAREB)
  • Chamber of Real Estate and Builders' Associations (CREBA)
  • Association of Realtors Cebu (ARC) - for Cebu-based brokers
  • Real Estate Service Practitioners Association of the Philippines (RESPAP)

Best Practices for Filipino Brokers

Client Communication Excellence

  • Respond to inquiries within 2 hours during business hours
  • Use multiple channels: SMS, Viber, WhatsApp, Messenger, email
  • Provide weekly updates to active clients
  • Be transparent about property conditions and pricing
  • Set realistic expectations about timelines

Property Showing Protocol

Schedule appointments in advance
Arrive 10 minutes early to prepare
Bring property documents and comps
Highlight unique features and benefits
Address concerns honestly
Follow up within 24 hours

Building Long-Term Client Relationships

  1. Send birthday and holiday greetings
  2. Share market updates and investment tips quarterly
  3. Offer property management assistance post-sale
  4. Request referrals and testimonials
  5. Maintain a CRM database of all clients
Filipino Touch: Remember special occasions, use "po" and "opo" appropriately, and consider cultural preferences when showing properties (feng shui, location near churches, etc.).

Negotiation Strategies

  • Know the property's true market value thoroughly
  • Understand both parties' motivations and timelines
  • Present multiple options, not ultimatums
  • Document all offers and counteroffers in writing
  • Stay neutral and professional, even in heated discussions
  • Know when to walk away from unreasonable deals
Commission Standard: Typical broker commission is 3-5% of sale price, split between buyer's and seller's agents. Always clarify commission structure upfront.

Due Diligence Checklist

Before any transaction, verify:

  1. Title authenticity at Registry of Deeds
  2. Tax declarations are updated and paid
  3. No liens or encumbrances on property
  4. Zoning compliance and land use classification
  5. Building permits for structures (if applicable)
  6. Association dues current (for condos/subdivisions)
  7. Seller's identity and authority to sell
Red Flags to Watch:
  • Seller refuses to show original title
  • Property taxes haven't been paid in years
  • Rushed transactions with unrealistic deadlines
  • Below-market pricing without clear reason
  • Seller not personally present during critical steps

Foreign Buyer Restrictions

  • Foreigners can own condominium units (max 40% foreign ownership per building)
  • Foreigners CANNOT own land in the Philippines
  • Option: Long-term lease (50 years, renewable for 25 years)
  • Option: Philippine corporation with 60% Filipino ownership

Ethical Guidelines

  • Never misrepresent property conditions or ownership
  • Disclose all known defects and issues
  • Maintain client confidentiality
  • Avoid conflicts of interest
  • Never practice law or give legal advice
  • Protect earnest money deposits in escrow

Transaction Process Guide

Step-by-Step Buying Process

  1. Client Consultation: Understand budget, preferences, timeline
  2. Property Search: Show 5-8 suitable options
  3. Property Viewing: Schedule and conduct tours
  4. Offer & Negotiation: Submit written offer, negotiate terms
  5. Reservation: Collect reservation fee (₱20,000-100,000)
  6. Due Diligence: Verify title, check property status
  7. Contract Signing: Contract to Sell or Deed of Sale
  8. Payment: Coordinate with bank for loan processing
  9. Title Transfer: Process at Registry of Deeds
  10. Turnover: Hand over keys and documents

Selling Process Timeline

  • Week 1: Property evaluation, pricing strategy, listing agreement
  • Week 2-3: Professional photos, marketing launch, property showings
  • Week 4-8: Active marketing, buyer negotiations, offers received
  • Week 9-10: Contract preparation, due diligence period
  • Week 11-16: Bank loan processing, title verification
  • Week 17-20: Final payments, title transfer, turnover

Common Payment Structures

Cash Payment:
  • Reservation: 10-20% of total price
  • Balance: Upon completion of documents (30-60 days)
  • Fastest process: 45-90 days total
Bank Financing:
  • Downpayment: 20-30% (can be paid in installments)
  • Bank loan: 70-80% of appraised value
  • Typical terms: 5-30 years at 6-10% interest
  • Processing: 60-90 days
Pag-IBIG Financing:
  • Maximum loan: ₱6,000,000
  • Interest rate: 5-7% (usually lower than banks)
  • Maximum term: 30 years
  • Must be Pag-IBIG member with 24 monthly contributions

Closing Costs Breakdown

Seller's Expenses:

  • Capital Gains Tax: 6% of selling price or zonal value
  • Documentary Stamp Tax: 1.5% of selling price
  • Transfer Tax: 0.5-0.75% (varies by LGU)
  • Real Estate Broker Commission: 3-5%
  • Notarial fees: ₱5,000-15,000

Buyer's Expenses:

  • Registration Fee: approximately 0.25% of property value
  • Documentary Stamp Tax: 1.5% (if not paid by seller)
  • Transfer Tax: 0.5-0.75% (if not paid by seller)
  • Real Estate Broker Commission: 2-3% (if separate buyer's agent)
  • Loan processing fees (if financed): 1-2% of loan amount
Important: Always discuss who pays what expenses upfront. Get agreement in writing to avoid disputes during closing.

Post-Sale Responsibilities

  • Ensure all documents are properly filed
  • Follow up on title transfer completion (60-90 days)
  • Provide utility transfer assistance to buyer
  • Request testimonial and referrals
  • Maintain relationship for future transactions
  • Keep transaction records for 7 years minimum

Property Types Deep Dive

Condominiums

Pre-Selling Units:

  • Lower prices: 15-30% discount vs ready units
  • Flexible payment terms (12-60 months to move-in)
  • Risk: construction delays common (6-12 months typical)
  • Higher appreciation potential before turnover
  • Requires: reservation (₱20K-50K), downpayment (10-20%)

Ready for Occupancy (RFO):

  • Immediate move-in after full payment
  • See actual unit condition before buying
  • Limited payment terms (usually cash or bank financing only)
  • Higher price but no waiting time
  • Better for investors needing immediate rental income
Condo Selling Points: Location near business districts, amenities (pool, gym, function rooms), security, parking slots, property management quality, association dues rate.

House & Lot Subdivisions

  • Best for families wanting privacy and space
  • Lot sizes: 50-500+ sqm typical
  • Price range: ₱2M-50M+ depending on location
  • Check: HOA rules, monthly dues, amenities (clubhouse, playground)
  • Verify: water source, flood history, road access
  • Common issues: illegal structures, unpaid dues, boundary disputes

Townhouses

  • Middle ground between condo and house & lot
  • Typical: 2-3 floors, 80-150 sqm floor area
  • Lower than house & lot but more space than condo
  • Shared walls with neighbors (check soundproofing)
  • Usually includes 1-2 parking slots
  • Popular in: Quezon City, Parañaque, Las Piñas, Cavite

Commercial Properties

Types:
  • Office spaces: ₱500-2,000/sqm monthly lease
  • Retail shops: ₱800-3,000/sqm monthly lease
  • Warehouses: ₱150-400/sqm monthly lease
  • Mixed-use buildings: residential + commercial

Commercial Property Considerations:

  • Higher commission: 5-10% typical
  • Longer sales cycle: 6-18 months average
  • Requires business permit verification
  • Check zoning: commercial use allowed?
  • Lease vs sale: commercial lease contracts 3-10 years
  • Important: foot traffic, visibility, parking, access

Agricultural/Farm Land

  • Must verify: land classification (agricultural, residential, commercial)
  • DAR clearance required if covered by CARP
  • Restrictions: foreigners cannot own, corporate ownership limited
  • Price: ₱300-5,000/sqm depending on location and development
  • Conversion to residential: requires HLURB/DHSUD approval
  • Investment potential: near expanding cities (Bulacan, Cavite, Laguna)
Farm Land Red Flags: No access road, disputed ownership, occupied by tenants/farmers (ARBs), landlocked property, unclear boundaries.

Foreclosed Properties

Advantages:

  • Below market price: 10-30% discount typical
  • Clean title (bank already processed foreclosure)
  • Various payment options available
  • No haggling: prices usually firm but negotiable

Disadvantages & Risks:

  • Sold "as is where is" - no repairs from bank
  • May need substantial repairs/renovation
  • Possible occupants (previous owners) - eviction needed
  • Limited viewing time and access
  • Competition: bidding process common for prime properties

Memorial Lots

  • Niche market but steady demand
  • Major players: Manila Memorial Park, Himlayang Pilipino, Heritage Park
  • Commission: 5-15% typical (higher than real estate)
  • Payment terms: installment plans available (6-36 months)

Crisis Management

Handling Deal Cancellations

Common Reasons:

  • Buyer loses financing/job loss
  • Title issues discovered
  • Buyer's remorse
  • Seller backs out for better offer

Immediate Actions:

  1. Stay calm and professional
  2. Review contract cancellation clauses
  3. Document everything
  4. Contact both parties separately
  5. Attempt negotiation before formal cancellation

Client Disputes

Undisclosed Defects:

  • Review your disclosure documents
  • If you didn't know: typically not liable
  • If you knew: serious ethical violation
  • Mediate repair cost negotiation

Commission Disputes:

  • Show signed listing agreement
  • Document all work performed
  • Escalate to firm's legal team if needed

Managing Difficult Clients

The Indecisive Buyer:

  • Set clear criteria, narrow to 3-5 options
  • Create decision matrix
  • Set deadline for decision

The Unrealistic Seller:

  • Show comparable sold properties
  • Present market data
  • Suggest trial period at their price
Know When to Walk Away: You can terminate relationships if clients: refuse to respect boundaries, become abusive, ask for illegal/unethical actions, or waste time with no intent to transact.

Title Issues

Title Not in Seller's Name:

  • Process: extrajudicial settlement, estate tax, transfer
  • Timeline: 3-6 months minimum
  • Cost: ₱50,000-200,000

Liens or Encumbrances:

  • Must be cleared before sale
  • Seller responsibility to settle
  • Option: Use sale proceeds to pay at closing

Fake Title:

  • STOP TRANSACTION IMMEDIATELY
  • Verify at Registry of Deeds
  • Report to authorities if fraud confirmed

Natural Disasters

Typhoons and Flooding:

  • Always check flood history
  • Review hazard maps
  • Disclosure required
  • Remind buyers about flood insurance

If Property Damaged Before Closing:

  • Review "risk of loss" clause in contract
  • Typically: Risk stays with seller until turnover
  • Options: Cancel, renegotiate price, wait for repairs

Prospecting & Lead Generation

Building Your Lead Pipeline

The 80/20 Rule: 80% of business from 20% of activities. Focus on: referrals, past clients, sphere of influence, online leads.

Cold Calling

Best Times:

  • Weekdays: 10am-12pm, 2pm-4pm
  • Saturdays: 10am-2pm
  • Make 20-30 calls daily
Opening: "Good morning po! This is [Name] from [Brokerage]. I'm a licensed real estate broker specializing in [area]. Do you have 2 minutes? I'd like to share some exciting property opportunities."

Door-to-Door

Approach:

  1. Dress professionally
  2. Bring business cards and flyers
  3. Best time: Saturday 10am-2pm
  4. Introduce yourself and offer value
  5. Leave materials if no one's home
Safety First: Door knock in pairs. Bring charged phone. Inform someone of your route. Trust your instincts.

Open Houses

Schedule 2 weeks in advance
Clean and stage property
Print property flyers (50+ copies)
Place directional signs
Prepare refreshments
Create sign-in sheet

Referral Programs

  • Past clients: 10-20% of commission on referrals
  • Other professionals: ₱10,000-50,000 per closed referral
  • Friends/family: Gift certificates, dinner vouchers

Online Lead Generation

Facebook Lead Ads:

  • Budget: ₱100-500/day
  • Target: 25-45 years old, interested in real estate
  • Cost per lead: ₱50-200
  • Respond within 5 minutes

Google Ads:

  • Higher cost but better quality: ₱300-800/lead
  • Active searchers (hot leads)
  • Budget: ₱200-1,000/day minimum

Prospecting Schedule

Daily (2-3 hours):
  • 20-30 cold calls (30-45 min)
  • 5-10 follow-up calls (30 min)
  • Social media engagement (30 min)
  • Email responses (30 min)
  • Database updates (15 min)

The Numbers:

  • 100 cold calls → 20 conversations → 5 appointments → 1 client
  • 50 online leads → 10 qualified → 3 showings → 1 offer

Building Your Brokerage Firm

Requirements:

  • Active PRC broker license
  • Register business with DTI or SEC
  • Get Mayor's Permit and business permits
  • BIR registration
  • DHSUD accreditation
  • Professional indemnity insurance

Startup Costs:

  • Business registration: ₱10,000-30,000
  • Office setup: ₱50,000-150,000
  • Marketing materials: ₱30,000-80,000
  • Working capital (6 months): ₱100,000-300,000
  • Total: ₱210,000-600,000 minimum
Don't rush! Make sure you have: (1) Consistent income for 2+ years, (2) Strong client database, (3) Emergency fund for 6-12 months, (4) Systems in place.

Hiring Agents

Commission Split Models:

  • Experienced agents: 60/40 or 70/30 (agent favor)
  • Top producers: 80/20 or 90/10

Desk Fee Model:

Property Management Services

Why Add It:

  • Recurring revenue: 10-15% of monthly rent
  • Income stability during slow sales
  • Keep relationship with investor clients

Real Estate Investing

Pre-Selling Flipping:
  • Buy pre-selling, sell before turnover
  • Capital: ₱200,000-500,000 for DP
  • Hold: 2-4 years
  • Expected gain: 20-50%
Buy and Hold Rental:
  • Purchase RFO, rent long-term
  • Target: 5-8% annual rental yield
  • Monthly cash flow + appreciation
  • Capital: ₱500K-2M for DP

Income Milestones

  • Year 2: ₱400K-800K (3-6 deals)
  • Year 3: ₱800K-1.5M (6-10 deals)
  • Year 5: ₱1.5M-3M (10-15 deals)
  • Year 10: ₱3M-8M+ (15-25+ deals)

Top 10% Brokers:

  • Earn ₱5M-15M+ annually
  • Specialize in luxury or commercial
  • Have team of 5-15 agents

Profitable Niches

  • Luxury Properties: ₱20M+ deals, higher commissions

  • Retirement Properties: Retirees and expats

Success Habits
